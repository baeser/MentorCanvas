Program Overview

Supporting new school counselors in their first years at DCS

🎯 Mission

The DCS New Counselor Mentor Program creates the conditions for new school counselors to thrive — building confidence in counseling practice, fostering well-being and belonging, deepening connection to our school community, and strengthening the comprehensive school counseling program. We believe new counselors grow best through trusting, collaborative, strengths-based mentoring relationships that honor each counselor's prior experience, professional identity, and individual goals.

🤝 The Mentor Role

Mentoring in DCS is non-supervisory, non-evaluative, and confidential. Mentors are not evaluators. The mentor's role is to support the new counselor's growth as a thought partner, sounding board, and trusted colleague — never to report on, judge, or assess them.

What's shared between mentor and mentee stays between them, with the only exceptions being situations involving student safety, mandatory reporting obligations, or clear ethical or legal concerns. The boundary matters even more for counselors than for teachers — counselor work involves confidential student and family information that must never be shared in the mentoring relationship. The mentor doesn't need protected case details to be useful.

📓 Mentor–Mentee Contact Log

Every mentor pair maintains a contact log throughout the year. Each meeting is a row: date, start and end times, topic or activity, and both mentor and mentee initials.

Building principal sign-off happens at two gate checks: November 15 for the first half (Aug–Nov) and May 1 for the second half (Dec–May), with year-end total hours filled in at the close. DCS requires three full years of mentoring for new counselors — the same contact log structure applies in Years 2 and 3.

The Four Domains of Mentoring Practice

Drawn from Michigan Standards 7–10, with counselor-specific sub-standards (7b, 8b, 9b, 10b). Each monthly module is built around these four domains.

🤝 Relationship & Goals Build trust, set goals together, review progress, and provide formative feedback aligned to the new counselor's priorities and ASCA-aligned practice.
💚 Well-Being & Belonging Sustain practice through the emotional weight of counseling work — vicarious stress, crisis exposure, and the boundary between professional empathy and personal absorption.
🌐 Culture & Community Connect new counselors to colleagues, families, MTSS teams, and the broader Dexter community; clarify the role across stakeholder groups.
📚 Counseling Practice Strengthen practice through ASCA National Model alignment, use-of-time analysis, Mindsets and Behaviors integration, and reflection.

🚨 Crisis Debriefing Protocol

Per Standard 8b.2, mentors offer structured debriefing after the new counselor encounters a high-stakes situation: a suicide assessment, a child protective services referral, a threat assessment, an acute family crisis, or any situation that activated the new counselor's nervous system.

The debrief is not a case review. The mentor does not need protected details. The debrief asks: How are you? What are you carrying? What do you need to put down before you go home? When are you next due to do something that touches this case, and what support do you want around that?

A standing offer to debrief — within 24–48 hours of the new counselor's request — is more important than a perfect debrief script. The mentee initiates; the mentor responds. The contact log notes the debrief took place without identifying details.

August

Onboarding, Role Clarification & ASCA Orientation

🌻

August Focus

August sets the tone for the mentoring relationship and for the new counselor's first year. The work this month is about establishing trust, learning who your mentee is, orienting them to DCS and to the program they're inheriting, and clarifying the role across stakeholders. Listen first; advise lightly.

⚠️ A Reminder About Your Role

You are not your mentee's evaluator. Your role is non-supervisory, non-evaluative, and confidential. For counselors, this also means you don't need — and shouldn't ask for — protected details about students or families on your mentee's caseload. The mentoring conversation is about them as a developing professional, not about specific cases.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership and beginning the goal-setting process.

•Have an initial conversation focused entirely on getting to know your mentee — their preparation pathway, prior experience, what drew them to school counseling, what they're excited about, what they're uncertain about
•Exchange contact information and agree on preferred communication, including a clear protocol for crisis-debrief requests
•Schedule recurring weekly meetings for August and September — protect this time
•Explicitly discuss confidentiality — both the standard non-evaluative boundary and the additional layer that you do not need protected case details
•Begin draft goal-setting: invite your mentee to name 2–3 areas they want to grow in this year — these will be refined in September against the ASCA National Model
•Create a shared calendar or doc for tracking meetings, ASCA audits, and goal progress

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being from day one.

•Talk openly about the emotional weight of counseling work — vicarious stress, holding hard student stories — and normalize that this is the profession, not personal weakness
•Help your mentee identify a "stop time" each evening and at least one full day per week with no school work
•Make introductions to closest collaborators — social worker, school psychologist, special ed staff, building admin team, front office, nurse
•Share district employee assistance and wellness resources; specifically name that counselors using EAP is healthy practice
•Connect your mentee to the Michigan School Counselor Association (MSCA) and ASCA — professional belonging beyond the building matters more for counselors because the role is often isolated within the building
•Make the standing crisis-debrief offer explicit: "If you have a hard one — a suicide assessment, a CPS call, an acute situation — text me. I'll meet you within 48 hours."

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community and the broader Dexter community.

•Walk through the counselor's office: privacy considerations, document storage, scheduling tools, where students sign in, where the box of tissues lives
•Introduce your mentee to building leadership, support staff, custodial staff, food service, and front office team — for counselors, the front office is a particularly important relationship
•Walk through entry/exit procedures, alarm codes, and especially the building's emergency response and lockdown protocols — counselors often play a defined role in emergencies
•Share context on the Dexter community — who our families are, what they value, key traditions, which student populations have specific support needs
•Review district policies (sick leave, social media expectations) — counselor social media boundaries with students and families warrant a specific conversation
•Discuss role clarification: what counseling is and isn't at DCS, how it's distinct from social work, school psych, and admin

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice from the start.

•Walk through the comprehensive school counseling program your mentee is inheriting — what exists on paper, what exists in practice
•Orient your mentee to the ASCA National Model — the four components (Define, Manage, Deliver, Assess) and how they organize the work
•Walk through technology: scheduling system, PowerSchool, counseling-specific data systems (504 documentation, suicide assessment forms, threat assessment tools)
•Discuss FERPA from the counselor's specific position — what can be shared, with whom, under what circumstances; the difference between counseling notes and educational records; mandated reporting obligations
•Review where to find IEPs, 504 plans, and CA60s; discuss the counselor's specific role in 504 coordination
•Walk through suicide assessment, threat assessment, and CPS reporting protocols — your mentee needs to know the procedure before they need it, not during
•Confirm with your mentee which framework or rubric is used for counselor evaluation at DCS
💡 Mentor Tip: The most powerful thing you can do in August is help your mentee not become the building's "everything-that-isn't-academics" person by week three. New counselors say yes to every request because they want to be useful — and by October they have no time for the work the role actually requires. Gently surface the skill of declining or redirecting requests that don't fit the counselor scope.

📓 Logging Reminder

Every meeting goes in the log: date, start and end times, topic, both initials. Crisis debriefs go in the log too — date, time, "crisis debrief" as the topic, both initials. No protected details. Build the habit now: log right after each meeting.

September

Goal-Setting, First Case Consultation & Year Planning

🍂

September Focus

August was about getting to know each other. September is about setting up the year. Finalize ASCA-aligned goals, walk through what a counselor's annual calendar looks like at this level, and run your first case consultation. The first few weeks reveal what the new counselor actually needs — adjust support accordingly.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Run your first case consultation. Have your mentee bring a non-identifiable scenario — a presenting situation, a question they're sitting with, a decision they're trying to make — and walk it through together. The conversation should be 80% mentee thinking out loud, 20% your noticings. This is the counselor equivalent of a first observation cycle: low-stakes, mentee-led, focused on growing the practitioner's reasoning, not solving the case.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership and beginning case consultation cycles.

•Finalize 2–3 goals together — written, specific enough to track, owned by the mentee, anchored to ASCA components or the counselor's professional growth
•Run the first case consultation (see Big Move) — let your mentee lead the framing, ask questions before offering analysis
•Confirm meeting cadence — weekly is appropriate for most Year 1 counselors
•Schedule October meetings before September ends — including space for the October ASCA Alignment Audit
•Log every meeting and case consultation

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being through the first wave of caseload pressure.

•Check in honestly on energy and stress — late September is when start-of-year adrenaline fades and the volume of student concerns starts to compound
•Watch for vicarious overload signs: difficulty sleeping, intrusive thoughts about specific students, irritability at home, withdrawal from staff interactions
•Help your mentee identify one strategy for "putting work down" — a closing-of-day ritual, a transition between work and home, a debrief habit with a trusted peer
•Celebrate concrete wins — name specific things you've seen them do well; counselor work is less visible than teaching
•Reaffirm the standing crisis-debrief offer; mention specifically that even "small" hard moments count

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community and clarifying the role across stakeholder groups.

•Walk through the MTSS structure at DCS — how the counselor fits into the intervention team, how referrals flow, what data drives Tier 2 and Tier 3 decisions
•Discuss how to handle teacher requests for counselor support — what's appropriate to consult on, what should become a referral, what falls outside the scope
•Walk through parent communication norms — what counselors typically reach out about, what gets routed through the teacher or admin, how to handle escalations
•Continue staff introductions — by end of September, your mentee should know names and roles of regular collaborators
•Mention upcoming building/district events and how counselors typically engage

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice through year planning and case consultation.

•Walk through the counselor's annual calendar at your level (elementary / middle / high) — what happens when, what's seasonal vs. ongoing
•Discuss the four ASCA components with concrete examples from your mentee's actual program: what does Define look like in their building? What's already documented under Manage?
•Walk through use-of-time analysis — direct vs. indirect services, the ASCA recommendation that 80%+ of time goes to direct/indirect student services, and how to begin tracking
•Review where the new counselor's caseload, scheduling responsibilities, and 504 coordination duties live (varies by level)
•Discuss the school discipline policy and counselor's role — what to consult on, what not to take on, where the boundary with admin lives
•Revisit specific protocols (suicide assessment, threat assessment, CPS reporting) now that real situations may have surfaced
💡 Mentor Tip: A good case consultation isn't a list of advice — it's a structured conversation. Try opening with: "Walk me through what you're seeing." Then: "What's already been tried?" Then: "What feels like the right next move, and what's making you hesitate?" Their answers tell you whether they need affirmation, a different lens, or just space to think out loud.

📓 Logging Reminder

Log every meeting and case consultation — "case consultation" is fine as the topic, no protected details. The November 15 gate check is six weeks away.

October

First ASCA Alignment Audit & MTSS Engagement

🎃

October Focus

October is the first real test of the new counselor's stamina. The September honeymoon is over, the volume of student concerns is real, and the program your mentee inherited is starting to feel like theirs to run. October is the right month for the first ASCA Alignment Audit — a diagnostic look at the program your mentee is operating inside before they try to change it — and for going deeper on MTSS engagement.

📌 This Month's Big Move

First ASCA Alignment Audit. Walk through the new counselor's program against the four ASCA components — Define, Manage, Deliver, Assess. What does the program assert it is for? What systems organize the work? What direct and indirect services are actually being delivered, in what proportions? How does the new counselor know any of it is working? The audit is diagnostic, not evaluative — it helps the new counselor see what they're operating inside, before they try to change it.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership and running the first ASCA audit.

•Maintain weekly meetings; if they've slipped, get back on track now
•Run the first ASCA Alignment Audit (see Big Move) — block 60–90 minutes; this isn't a 20-minute conversation
•Revisit the September goals — are they still pointing in the right direction now that reality has set in? Adjust if needed
•Schedule November meetings; flag the November 15 gate check
•Continue logging every meeting and the audit

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being — catching the first wave of vicarious overload.

•Ask directly: "What are you carrying home that you don't want to be carrying?" — then listen without immediately problem-solving
•Watch for the "I should be able to handle this" pattern — counselors apply professional empathy to others while being self-critical about their own struggle
•Talk through the parent-conference season from a counselor's seat — what counselors typically attend, how to prepare for hard family meetings
•Specifically name what you've seen them do well — your mentee may not see their own progress, especially because the work is largely invisible
•Encourage at least one fully unplugged weekend day before any conference week

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community — family engagement and the rhythms of fall.

•Walk through how counselors engage with parent-teacher conference week — drop-in availability, scheduled family meetings, post-conference referrals
•Role-play a difficult family conversation: an adversarial parent, a parent in crisis, a parent who wants the counselor to do something outside their scope
•Discuss when to loop in the social worker, school psychologist, admin, or outside community resources — and when not to
•Share context on Dexter families — what tends to surface in fall conferences, what local resources are available
•Review weather/closure protocols — especially how counseling appointments and crisis coverage are handled on disrupted days

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice through the ASCA audit, MTSS, and refining service delivery.

•Run the ASCA Alignment Audit (see Big Move) — document the takeaways together, but keep the spirit diagnostic, not corrective
•Look at use-of-time data with your mentee, even if rough — what proportion is going to direct services, indirect services, "fair share" duties, and program management?
•Discuss MTSS engagement at the next level of detail — Tier 2 small-group programming, Tier 3 referral signals, how to document interventions
•Revisit specific 504 cases now that accommodations are in real practice — what's working, what isn't
•Help your mentee identify one structural issue worth tracking through the year (referral patterns, group attendance, scheduling bottlenecks) and decide what year-end data they'd want
•Conduct one Mindsets and Behaviors integration check — pick one or two M&Bs your mentee has intentionally targeted and walk through how they've shown up
💡 Mentor Tip: When the ASCA Alignment Audit surfaces a gap — and it will — resist the urge to make it a fix-it list. New counselors cannot rebuild the program in their first year, and shouldn't try. The audit's value is in helping your mentee see what they inherited so they can hold it accurately. The improvement work happens later, in second-year goal-setting, with eyes wide open.

📓 Logging Reminder

The November 15 gate check is roughly four weeks away. If your log is current, it's a quick principal review. If it isn't, now is the time to reconstruct what's missing.

November

Mid-Year Reflection & First Gate Check

🍁

November Focus

November is the first gate check — both for the program (Nov 15 sign-off) and for the new counselor (almost a full quarter in). Shift your mentee from being supported to looking sideways: how are other counselors handling the same pressures? A pair shadow or a structured visit to another counselor (in-district or in another district) is more useful in November than in any other month.

🚨 Gate Check Deadline: November 15

By November 15, your building principal needs to initial the first half (Aug–Nov) of your contact log. This is a checkpoint, not the documentation itself — your ongoing log is what feeds it. If you've been filling in the log after each meeting, this is a quick principal signature. If not, build in time to reconstruct before you bring it to your principal.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Arrange one structured visit or shadow with another counselor — either a counselor in another DCS building (different level if possible) or a counselor in a neighboring district. Your mentee names what they want to look at in advance; the visit is followed by a debrief where they lead what they noticed.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership and submitting the Nov 15 gate check.

•Bring your contact log to your building principal for first-half initials before November 15
•Conduct a structured progress review on the September goals: what's working, what isn't, what should be adjusted
•Maintain weekly meetings — the holiday season will tempt you to skip; don't
•Schedule December meetings, including a check-in before winter break
•Continue logging every meeting

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being — acknowledging fatigue and protecting the holiday season.

•Name what you've seen them do well since August — concretely. New counselors in November are running on fumes and rarely see their own growth
•Talk through Thanksgiving break — encourage genuine disconnection, not "catch-up" time on caseload notes
•Watch for the post-holiday-uptick concern — counselors often see disclosure spikes after Thanksgiving as families gather; help your mentee anticipate the December workload
•Check in on whether your mentee has a peer (besides you) they feel safe vending to about hard days — counselor isolation is structural and worth surfacing

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community — following up on conferences and continuing to build community ties.

•Debrief October's conference week from the counselor seat: which families surfaced concerns that became referrals, what the post-conference workload looks like
•Help your mentee draft any follow-up communications to families before they sit too long
•Discuss inclusive holiday practices in counseling — how to support students whose family situations make the season hard, how to handle holiday-themed activities elsewhere in the building that may not land for everyone
•Continue connecting your mentee to colleagues; by November they should feel they belong, not just that they showed up

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice — learning by watching through counselor shadow and reflection.

•Arrange the counselor shadow / structured visit (see Big Move)
•Debrief the shadow together: what did they notice about workflow, decision-making, time use, family communication, what might they try in their own program
•Run another case consultation if a real situation has surfaced — by November there usually is one
•Review report card / progress report cycle for your level — counselors typically get pulled into family communication around grades that surprise; help your mentee anticipate
•Address documentation patterns — by November, your mentee should have a workable system for case notes, group attendance, and 504 meeting records
💡 Mentor Tip: When your mentee shadows another counselor, they will almost always come back saying "they're doing it so much better than I am." Help them reframe: every counselor's program looks different from the inside than from the outside, and what they're observing is months of accumulated practice in a building they don't know. The point of the visit isn't to compare; it's to expand the sense of what's possible.

📓 Logging Reminder

If your log has gaps, fix them before submitting the Nov 15 form. Going forward, log right after each meeting — December's holiday distractions will eat any habit that isn't already automatic.

December

Crisis-Readiness, Semester Closure & Well-Being

❄️

December Focus

December for counselors is the heaviest emotional month of the year. Family stress compounds, students disclose more, the building gets loud and unstructured, and the volume of crisis-adjacent situations spikes. Your job this month is to keep your mentee functional through the surge and make sure they actually rest at the break. This is not the month for new initiatives.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Have one explicit conversation about how your mentee will rest over winter break. Counselors specifically struggle with this because they carry concern for specific students who they know are heading into hard family situations. Help your mentee distinguish between productive concern and rumination — and make a concrete plan for what they'll do with the worry over break.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — closing the semester with a brief but real reflection.

•Hold a short semester-closing reflection: what's gone better than expected, what's been hardest, what's worth carrying into second semester
•Schedule a pre-break touchpoint and a first-week-of-January meeting before everyone scatters
•Skip new structural work this month — keep the focus on getting through the semester intact
•Continue logging — even short check-ins count

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being — the most important domain of the month.

•Have the rest conversation (see Big Move) — make a concrete plan, not a vague intention
•Help your mentee identify which students they are appropriately worried about and which they are over-functioning for — counselors specifically take responsibility for outcomes that aren't theirs to own
•Acknowledge holiday stressors that have nothing to do with school: family obligations, financial pressure, grief, loneliness — December exhaustion compounds with all of it
•Celebrate what they've actually accomplished — by name, in specifics. They will not give themselves credit; you have to
•If you sense real struggle (persistent low affect, hopelessness, talk of leaving the profession), gently surface it and connect them to district employee assistance resources
•Reaffirm the standing crisis-debrief offer — December is when it gets used most

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community — inclusive practice during a culturally loaded month.

•Discuss inclusive holiday practices in counseling — how to support students whose family situations make the season hard, how to handle holiday-themed activities that may not land for everyone
•Walk through the heightened reporting environment of December — disclosures often spike before and after break; review CPS reporting flow, suicide assessment thresholds, weekend coverage
•Walk through how counselor coverage works over break — what's in place for student crises during closure, who carries the after-hours phone, how a hand-off back happens in January
•Talk through gift-giving norms — what's appropriate to accept from families, how to respond when students give nothing

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice — closing the semester cleanly without overloading the runway.

•Walk through the second-semester scheduling work that is about to land (course requests, schedule changes, transitions in or out)
•Help finalize first-semester documentation — case notes, group attendance, 504 records, anything that needs to close out before break
•Encourage your mentee to document one or two interventions that worked — they'll want this in January when re-energy is needed
•Confirm any small-group programming pausing or restarting in January is set up — group rosters, parent communications, room reservations
•Run another Mindsets and Behaviors integration check — quarterly cadence per Standard 10b.2
💡 Mentor Tip: Send a short message at the start of break — "Have a great break, see you in January, no need to reply." It signals that you're a steady presence, not another inbox they need to manage. Then actually leave them alone until January, unless they reach out for a debrief. The standing crisis-debrief offer doesn't pause for break.

📓 Logging Reminder

Even brief, informal check-ins count and should be logged. December meetings tend to be shorter and more frequent — log them all, even if the entry is two lines. Crisis debriefs over break go in the log too.

January

Mid-Year Goal Review & Second-Semester Reset

🎊

January Focus

January is a real opportunity to reset — but reset doesn't mean restart. Help your mentee build on what's working in their counseling practice while honestly identifying what needs to change. The first semester's data is now real (caseload patterns, group attendance, referral flow, family relationships). Use it.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Conduct a structured mid-year goal review against the goals you set in September. What's been accomplished, what's been quietly abandoned, what should be revised, and what new goal should emerge for second semester? Document the updated goals.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — mid-year goal revision and a second case consultation.

•Hold the structured mid-year goal review (see Big Move) — write the updated goals down so they're as concrete as the originals
•Run a second case consultation, ideally tied to the revised goals or a current presenting situation
•Resume weekly meetings; the cadence matters more than the duration
•Schedule meetings through March
•Continue logging

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8b: Sustaining counseling practice and well-being — returning rested and staying that way.

•Genuinely ask how break went — and listen to the answer
•Help your mentee identify one or two non-negotiable boundaries for second semester (a hard stop in the evening, a no-work weekend day, a debrief habit with a peer)
•Celebrate the milestone — they made it through one full semester as a school counselor, which most people never do
•Watch for any indicators that the break didn't actually serve as a break — flat affect, dread about returning, talk of leaving the profession
•Encourage involvement in MSCA / ASCA programming if they aren't yet plugged in

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9b: Connecting your mentee to the school community — re-establishing community after the break.

•Help your mentee re-establish caseload and group routines after break — students often need them retaught after time away
•Walk through any new students entering second semester — transcripts, IEPs/504s, prior counseling records, family communication
•Discuss any DCS-wide changes for second semester — schedule shifts, new initiatives that affect counselor workflow
•Encourage your mentee to send a brief "looking ahead to second semester" communication to families on their active list

📚 Counseling Practice

Standard 10b: Strengthening comprehensive school counseling practice — using first-semester data to inform second-semester practice.

•Look at first-semester data together — caseload composition, group attendance, referral patterns, who's been seen and who hasn't, where time has gone. What is the data telling you?
