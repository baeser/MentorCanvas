Program Overview

Supporting new administrators through their first three years

🎯 Mission

The DCS New Administrator Mentor Program creates the conditions for new building and district leaders to thrive — building confidence in leadership practice, fostering well-being and belonging through cross-district networks, deepening connection to our school community, and strengthening instructional leadership. We believe new administrators grow best through trusting, collaborative, strengths-based mentoring relationships that honor each leader's prior experience and the realities of leading inside Dexter Community Schools.

🤝 The Mentor Role — and Its Particular Tension for Admins

Mentoring in DCS is non-supervisory, non-evaluative, and confidential. Mentors are not evaluators. The mentor's role is to support the new administrator's growth as a thought partner, sounding board, and trusted colleague — never to report on, judge, or assess them.

For administrators, this boundary requires extra care. Your mentor is often a peer in district leadership with overlapping authority, attendance at the same cabinet meetings, or a hand in decisions that affect the new administrator's building. The mentoring relationship is held separate from those structural dynamics. Things shared in mentoring stay in mentoring. The mentor does not carry the new administrator's struggles into evaluation conversations or leadership team discussions.

The only exceptions to confidentiality are situations involving student safety, staff safety, mandatory reporting obligations, or clear ethical or legal concerns. Otherwise, the relationship is genuinely confidential — and naming this explicitly at the start of the year is essential.

📓 Mentor–Mentee Contact Log

Every mentor pair maintains a contact log throughout the year. Each meeting is a row: date, start and end times, topic or activity, and both mentor and mentee initials.

Sign-off happens at two gate checks: November 15 for the first half (Aug–Nov) and May 1 for the second half (Dec–May), with year-end total hours filled in at the close. For administrators, the Human Resources Director signs off rather than a building principal — the new administrator typically is the building principal, or reports directly to a superintendent who would be inappropriate as the gate-check signer.

The Four Domains of Mentoring Practice

Drawn from Michigan Standards 7–10, with administrator-specific sub-standards (7c, 8c, 9c, 10c). Each monthly module is built around these four domains.

🤝 Relationship & Goals Build trust, set goals together, review progress, and provide formative feedback aligned to the new administrator's leadership priorities and NELP-aligned practice.
💚 Well-Being & Belonging Sustain practice through the isolation, decision fatigue, and political weight of leadership work. Cross-district peer networks (per Standard 8c.1) are central, not optional.
🌐 Culture & Community Connect new administrators to staff, families, the school board, and the broader Dexter community; navigate the political and relational dimensions of the role.
📚 Instructional Leadership Strengthen leadership practice — teacher evaluation, walkthrough practice, instructional decision-making, supervision, and how to support teaching without being the only voice that matters.

A Three-Year Journey

Per Michigan law (MCL 380.1249b), new administrators receive mentoring across their first three years. Support is most intensive in Year 1 and gradually shifts toward independence. The contract year for administrators is typically twelve months — mentoring continues through summer in a lighter form.

Year 1: Foundation First everything — first opening, first board meeting, first eval cycle, first hard parent call, first hiring round. Most intensive support.
Year 2: Refinement Deepening leadership practice with a year of experience as comparison. Emerging leadership beyond the building. More autonomy in goal-setting.
Year 3: Independence Mentee-led growth with consultative mentor support. Leadership trajectory beyond the program is the focus. MCL 380.1249b compliance closes here.

🌐 Cross-District Peer Networking — Required, Not Optional

Per Standard 8c.1, administrator well-being depends on networks beyond the home district. New administrators in their first years are uniquely isolated — there is often no one in their building who shares the role, and the people who do share it across the district may also be in their evaluation chain. Cross-district peers fill that gap.

The mentor's job is to make sure the new administrator is connected to:

•MASSP (Michigan Association of Secondary School Principals), MEMSPA (Michigan Elementary and Middle School Principals Association), or MASA (Michigan Association of Superintendents and Administrators) — depending on role and level
•NASSP or NAESP — national-level networks for principals
•The Washtenaw ISD administrator network — periodic regional convenings of admins across the ISD
•An informal cohort of 2–4 admins from neighboring districts at the same level who meet a few times a year

August

Opening, Late Hires, All-Staff PD & Establishing Presence

🌻

August Focus

August is the heaviest month of the year for a new administrator. The building opens, late vacancies need to be filled, all-staff PD has to land, and your mentee is forming their first impressions on every staff member at once. Your job this month is to be available, help them triage, and resist the urge to make decisions for them. Most decisions can be made by the new administrator with a thinking partner — they need help with sequencing, not with answers.

⚠️ A Reminder About Your Role

You are not your mentee's evaluator. Your role is non-supervisory, non-evaluative, and confidential. For administrators specifically, name explicitly that what's shared in mentoring stays in mentoring — it doesn't travel into cabinet meetings, evaluation conversations, or informal hallway debriefs with other district leaders.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership and beginning the goal-setting process.

•Have an initial conversation focused entirely on getting to know your mentee — leadership pathway, prior roles, what drew them to administration at DCS, what they're worried about
•Exchange contact information; agree on a fast-response protocol for high-stakes situations (a board surprise, a staff incident, a media question)
•Schedule recurring meetings — most Year 1 admins do well with weekly or bi-weekly, depending on what their role demands
•Explicitly walk through the confidentiality boundary, especially the parts that are unique to admin mentoring (the overlapping authority piece)
•Begin draft goal-setting: 2–3 areas the new administrator wants to grow in — drawn from their own sense of priorities, anchored to NELP standards if helpful but not as a checklist
•Create a shared calendar or doc for tracking meetings and key dates (board meetings, eval cycles, budget milestones)

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8c: Sustaining leadership practice and well-being from day one.

•Talk openly about the realistic intensity of an admin August — 12-hour days are common, the volume is real, exhaustion is normal not a sign of failure
•Help your mentee identify a sustainable pace early — what time they'll leave the building most days, when phone goes off, how weekends work
•Connect them to MASSP, MEMSPA, MASA, or NASSP/NAESP — whichever fits the role. Cross-district peer networks are required (Standard 8c.1), not optional
•Help them identify 2–3 admins at the same level in neighboring districts they should reach out to in the first six weeks
•Share district employee assistance and wellness resources
•Watch for the urge to perform certainty in front of staff that the new administrator doesn't actually have — admins who pretend to know everything in August often pay for it in October

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9c: Connecting your mentee to staff, families, and the broader community and the dynamics that will define the year.

•Help your mentee prepare for the all-staff opening — first impression matters, and "prepared and humble" beats "polished" almost every time
•Walk through the building leadership team your mentee inherits — strengths of each person, roles, prior dynamics, who's been here a while and who's newer
•Discuss the Board of Education — meeting cadence, what shows up on agendas, how the new administrator's role intersects with board work, who carries communication to the board
•Walk through union dynamics with the local DEA chapter — leadership, key contractual provisions affecting building decisions, who to call when something contractual surfaces
•Share context on the Dexter community — what families value, key traditions, what historically lands well or badly in family-facing communications
•Discuss social media presence and visibility expectations — what's appropriate, what district norms exist, what to avoid

📚 Instructional Leadership

Standard 10c: Strengthening leadership practice — instructional supervision, evaluation, and decision-making from the start.

•Walk through the teacher evaluation process at DCS — timeline, rubric, observation expectations, what gets formally documented vs. coached informally
•Discuss walkthrough practice — when, how often, what the new administrator is looking for, what the patterns of feedback look like in different staff cultures
•Review the master schedule, course load distribution, and staffing assignments — what your mentee inherited, where there's flexibility for January and beyond
•Walk through any building-level data that shapes priorities — achievement data, attendance, discipline, climate survey results, MTSS data
•Discuss the hiring process — how DCS posts, screens, interviews, and selects; the new administrator's role in late-summer hires
•Review building-level systems for IEP/504 oversight — the new administrator's responsibility for IDEA compliance, how special ed staff coordinate, where the principal sits in 504 disputes
•Confirm the framework or rubric used to evaluate this administrator's own performance — the new administrator should know what they're being evaluated against, separate from the mentoring relationship
💡 Mentor Tip: The most powerful thing you can do in August is help your mentee resist the urge to fix things before they understand them. Every system in the building was built by someone for a reason that may not be obvious. Encourage the August posture of "I'm watching how this works" rather than "here's what I'd change." The change can come in January, with eyes open.

📓 Logging Reminder

Every meeting goes in the log: date, start and end times, topic, both initials. The Human Resources Director signs off at the November 15 and May 1 gate checks. Build the habit now: log right after each meeting.

Topics include: Bloodborne Pathogens, FERPA, Sexual Harassment Prevention, and other required compliance modules.

September

First Walkthroughs, Goal-Setting & Board Cycle Awareness

🍂

September Focus

August was about getting open. September is about starting the actual work — visible in classrooms, finalizing leadership goals, and beginning to understand the rhythm of board meetings, cabinet, and the eval cycle that's about to start in November. The first board meeting of the year falls in September; help your mentee prepare for it.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Begin a regular walkthrough practice. Help your mentee schedule and protect time in classrooms — short visits, focused on a specific lens (engagement, formative assessment, climate). The point isn't to evaluate; it's to be visible, learn the building from the classroom level, and build relational capital with teachers before the first formal observation cycle starts in November.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — finalizing goals and beginning the rhythm.

•Finalize 2–3 leadership goals together — written, specific enough to track, owned by the mentee, anchored to NELP standards or to identified building priorities
•Confirm meeting cadence and protect it through September's busy stretch
•Schedule October meetings before September ends
•Have one structured conversation about what success looks like for your mentee at the end of Year 1 — concrete, not aspirational
•Continue logging every meeting

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8c: Sustaining leadership practice and well-being — sustaining through the first wave.

•Check in honestly on energy and stress — late September is when the first wave of staff issues, parent concerns, and operational fires arrives
•Watch for the "I should have anticipated that" pattern — admins specifically self-criticize for not predicting things that nobody could have predicted
•Help your mentee identify which decisions actually need their attention vs. which can be delegated or deferred
•Confirm cross-district peer connections are happening — at least one informal coffee or call by end of September
•Specifically name what you've seen them do well — admins rarely get unprompted positive feedback from staff in their first year

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9c: Connecting your mentee to staff, families, and the broader community — board, cabinet, and the political surface.

•Walk through preparation for the first board meeting — what the new administrator presents (if anything), how they sit, what gets surfaced before the meeting and what doesn't
•Discuss cabinet and district-level meetings — who attends, what the new administrator's voice should be in their first months, what to listen for
•Help your mentee understand the unwritten rules of district leadership — how disagreements are handled, what gets decided in the room vs. in side conversations, who actually holds influence
•Continue staff introductions and visibility — by end of September, the new administrator should have had at least a brief conversation with every staff member they supervise
•Talk through any community-facing event coming up (Homecoming, family nights) and what the administrator's presence should look like

📚 Instructional Leadership

Standard 10c: Strengthening leadership practice — instructional supervision, evaluation, and decision-making — walkthrough practice and instructional rhythm.

•Begin the regular walkthrough practice (see Big Move) — short, focused visits, scheduled into the calendar like any other commitment
•After early walkthroughs, debrief together: what is your mentee noticing about teaching across the building, what patterns are emerging, what is staff culture telling them
•Review the building's instructional priorities — what was set last year by the predecessor, what your mentee wants to carry forward, what they want to revisit
•Discuss how to handle the first request for instructional guidance from a teacher — when to direct, when to coach, when to defer to instructional coaches or department leads
•Walk through the teacher evaluation calendar — when formal observation cycles begin, what pre-conference looks like, what the new administrator's pace will be
💡 Mentor Tip: When your mentee tells you something a staff member said that frustrated them, resist the urge to validate the frustration. Ask: "What do you think is going on for them?" Helping the new admin shift from staff-as-obstacle to staff-as-context is one of the most important moves of Year 1 — and it almost always comes from a mentor asking the right question.

📓 Logging Reminder

Log every meeting and walkthrough debrief. The November 15 gate check is six weeks away. If you log as you go, sign-off takes minutes.

October

First Parent Escalation, Conferences & Staff Support

🎃

October Focus

October is when the first parent escalation arrives in your mentee's office, the first staff conflict that won't resolve itself surfaces, and parent-teacher conferences put extra weight on the building. Your job this month is to help your mentee handle the hard parent call without overcorrecting, support staff through conferences, and notice early signs of decision fatigue.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Walk your mentee through how to handle a hard parent escalation before one lands — including the structure of the conversation, when to bring in central office, when to defer to the teacher, and how to follow up. A 30-minute role-play of a tough parent meeting will save them hours of anxiety and prevent at least one regrettable email.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — sustaining the partnership and running a mid-month check-in.

•Maintain agreed cadence; if it's slipped, get back on track now
•Revisit the September goals — are they still pointing in the right direction now that reality has set in?
•Schedule November meetings; flag the November 15 gate check
•Continue logging

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8c: Sustaining leadership practice and well-being — catching the first wave of decision fatigue.

•Ask directly: "What's the hardest part of your week right now?" — then listen without immediately problem-solving
•Watch for the volume of small decisions overwhelming your mentee — one of the most common Year 1 admin patterns is exhaustion not from any single hard decision but from the cumulative weight of dozens of small ones
•Help triage: what genuinely needs their attention this week vs. what could wait, be delegated, or be answered with a brief "check with X"
•Talk through the emotional weight of conferences from an admin's seat — being available for hard family meetings, fielding conference fallout, supporting staff who had rough conversations
•Specifically name what you've seen them do well — Year 1 admins rarely see their own progress
•Reaffirm the cross-district peer network — by October, at least one connection should be a real relationship, not just an exchanged email

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9c: Connecting your mentee to staff, families, and the broader community — family escalations and the political surface.

•Walk through how to handle the first hard parent escalation (see Big Move)
•Role-play a difficult conversation: an angry parent, a parent who wants something the school can't or won't do, a parent threatening to escalate to the board
•Coach on the structure of a productive parent meeting: listen first, name what you heard, share what you can do and what you can't, agree on a follow-up
•Discuss when to loop in central office (HR Director, superintendent, legal) — and when not to. Calling in too early erodes credibility; calling in too late creates a mess
•Walk through parent-teacher conference logistics from the admin's seat — building presence, fielding hard conversations, supporting staff who get pulled into them
•Discuss any community context that may surface in conferences — local family dynamics, recent community events that affect family mood

📚 Instructional Leadership

Standard 10c: Strengthening leadership practice — instructional supervision, evaluation, and decision-making — supporting staff through conferences and refining walkthrough practice.

•Continue regular walkthroughs; by October the practice should be visible to staff and predictable
•Help your mentee notice what walkthrough patterns are showing — which classrooms are humming, which feel off, what supports might be needed before the formal eval cycle starts in November
•Discuss how to support staff who had rough conferences — drop-by check-ins, helping draft follow-up communications, surfacing the kind of administrative backup that builds staff trust
•Walk through any teacher who's already showing signs of struggle — what conversation needs to happen, when, by whom (the admin, an instructional coach, a peer mentor)
•Begin preparing for the formal teacher evaluation cycle that starts in November — pre-conference templates, observation calendar, post-conference structures
💡 Mentor Tip: When your mentee gets their first really hard parent call, resist the urge to immediately strategize. Listen first — they need to vent before they need to problem-solve, and they probably need both more than they need a solution. Ask: "What's making this one stick?" The answer is usually the most useful piece of the conversation.

📓 Logging Reminder

The November 15 gate check is roughly four weeks away. If your log is current, it's a quick HR Director review. If it isn't, this is the time to reconstruct what's missing.

November

First Gate Check & First Formal Eval Cycle Begins

🍁

November Focus

November carries two big things: the Nov 15 gate check, and the start of the formal teacher evaluation cycle. The eval cycle is the moment your mentee transitions from being visible in classrooms (walkthroughs) to making formal judgments about practice. Help them get the first cycle right — pre-conference, observation, post-conference — because the patterns set in November tend to hold for the rest of the year.

🚨 Gate Check Deadline: November 15

By November 15, your Human Resources Director needs to initial the first half (Aug–Nov) of your contact log. This is a checkpoint, not the documentation itself — your ongoing log feeds it. If you've been filling in the log after each meeting, this is a quick HR signature. If not, build in time to reconstruct before submission.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Walk through the first formal teacher observation cycle in detail before it happens — the pre-conference, the observation itself, the post-conference. The goal is for the new administrator to enter that first observation with a clear plan and exit it having given the teacher useful, growth-oriented feedback that's also evaluation-defensible.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — gate check and eval cycle preparation.

•Bring your contact log to the HR Director for first-half initials before November 15
•Hold a structured progress review on the September goals — what's working, what isn't, what should be adjusted
•Maintain meetings through the holiday rhythm; the cadence will be tempting to skip in mid-November
•Schedule December meetings, including a check-in before winter break
•Continue logging

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8c: Sustaining leadership practice and well-being — acknowledging fatigue and protecting the holiday season.

•Name what you've seen them do well since August — concretely. New admins in November are running on fumes and rarely see their own growth
•Talk through Thanksgiving break — encourage genuine disconnection, not "catch-up on email" time
•Watch for emotional flatness or cynicism, which often appear in November after the post-conference letdown and before the first eval cycle starts
•Check in on whether your mentee has a peer (besides you and the cross-district network) they feel safe vending to about hard days — the loneliness of the role is structural and worth surfacing
•Confirm the cross-district peer network is being used — by November, at least monthly contact with at least two peers

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9c: Connecting your mentee to staff, families, and the broader community — following up on conferences and beginning the second board cycle.

•Debrief October's conferences from the admin perspective — what landed, what didn't, which families need follow-up, what staff feedback they got about admin support
•Help your mentee draft any follow-up communications to families before they sit too long
•Discuss inclusive holiday practices in building communications — how to acknowledge the season without alienating families who don't celebrate the same things
•Walk through November's board meeting — agenda items, the new administrator's role, anything coming up that requires preparation
•Continue connecting your mentee to colleagues; by November they should feel embedded in the leadership team, not just on it

📚 Instructional Leadership

Standard 10c: Strengthening leadership practice — instructional supervision, evaluation, and decision-making — running the first formal observation cycle.

•Walk through the first formal observation cycle (see Big Move) — pre-conference structure, observation focus, post-conference framing
•Help your mentee anticipate what kinds of feedback land well in DCS staff culture and which kinds create friction — useful information regardless of whether they choose to adjust
•Discuss how to handle a teacher who pushes back on feedback — staying anchored to the rubric without becoming defensive, knowing when to bring HR into the conversation
•Address any teacher already showing signs of struggle who should enter a more structured improvement process — Year 1 admins often delay this conversation; pushing it past November makes it harder, not easier
•Discuss report card / grading-cycle communications and the admin's role when grades surprise families
💡 Mentor Tip: When your mentee finishes their first formal observation, resist the urge to ask whether they "got it right." Ask instead: "What did you notice about your own posture in the post-conference?" The first observation usually teaches the new admin more about themselves than about the teacher. Help them name what they learned about how they show up under pressure.

📓 Logging Reminder

If your log has gaps, fix them before the Nov 15 gate check. Going forward, log right after each meeting — December's holiday distractions will eat any habit that isn't already automatic.

  • MOECS: Remind your mentee to log in to MOECS (Michigan Online Educator Certification System) and verify their educator certificate information is current — annual review, November is the right time to check.

December

Semester Closure, Holiday Community & Well-Being

❄️

December Focus

December for an admin is short, fragmented, and emotionally loud. Students are wired, staff are tired, families are stressed, and the building runs at higher volume than any other month. This is not the month for new initiatives. It's the month to help your mentee close the semester cleanly, support staff through the close, and actually rest at the break.

📌 This Month's Big Move

Have one explicit conversation about how your mentee will rest over winter break. Admins specifically struggle with this because their inbox doesn't pause, parents email through the holidays, and there's always a building issue to worry about. Help them make a concrete plan for how email gets handled, what gets handed off, and when work actually stops.

🤝 Relationship & Goals

Standard 7: Building a supportive, trusting partnership — closing the semester with a brief but real reflection.

•Hold a short semester-closing reflection: what's gone better than expected, what's been hardest, what's worth carrying into second semester
•Schedule a single pre-break touchpoint and a first-week-of-January meeting before everyone scatters
•Skip new structural work this month
•Continue logging — even short check-ins count

💚 Well-Being & Belonging

Standard 8c: Sustaining leadership practice and well-being — the most important domain of the month.

•Have the rest conversation (see Big Move) — concrete plan, not vague intention
•Help your mentee separate work that genuinely needs to be finished before break from work they're telling themselves needs to be finished — there's almost always a category collapse
•Acknowledge that holiday family obligations stack on top of December exhaustion in ways that aren't fair but are real
•Celebrate what they've actually accomplished — by name, in specifics. They will not give themselves credit; you have to
•If you sense real struggle (persistent low affect, hopelessness, talk of leaving the role), gently surface it and connect them to district employee assistance resources
•Confirm the cross-district peer network has had a December check-in — peer support over break matters

🌐 Culture & Community

Standard 9c: Connecting your mentee to staff, families, and the broader community — staff support and inclusive practice.

•Discuss how the new administrator can support staff through December — visibility in classrooms, gestures of appreciation that are real and not performative, being present in the building
•Walk through inclusive holiday practices — how to acknowledge the season in building communications, what staff celebrations look like, what to avoid
•Talk through end-of-semester routines for securing the building — what happens to the building over break, who's on call, how emergencies route
•Review winter weather protocols — closures, delays, communication chain — before the first one happens
•Discuss December's board meeting (if there is one) — what the new administrator presents, what to expect

📚 Instructional Leadership

Standard 10c: Strengthening leadership practice — instructional supervision, evaluation, and decision-making — closing the semester cleanly without overloading the runway.

•Coach on managing the building's energy in the days before break — the work still needs to happen, but engagement strategies matter more than content density
•Help your mentee plan how the semester closes for staff — final faculty meeting, semester-end communications, anything that needs to be tied off
•Walk through semester grade submission timelines and any escalations admins might be pulled into when grades surprise families
•Encourage your mentee to document one or two leadership moves that worked particularly well this semester — they'll want this in January when re-energy is needed
•Confirm any teacher in the formal eval cycle has clear next steps documented before break
💡 Mentor Tip: Send a short message at the start of break — "Have a great break, see you in January, no need to reply." It signals you're a steady presence, not another inbox they need to manage. Then actually leave them alone until January, unless they reach out.

📓 Logging Reminder

Even brief, informal check-ins count and should be logged. December meetings tend to be shorter — log them all, even if the entry is two lines.

January

Mid-Year Reset & Budget Cycle Begins

🎊

January Focus

January is a real opportunity to reset — but it's also the month the budget cycle begins in earnest. Your mentee will be balancing reconnecting with staff after break, mid-year goal review, and starting to think about what next year's building or department needs in financial terms.
